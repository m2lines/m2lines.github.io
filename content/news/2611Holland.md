Led by **Marika Holland**, this **[study](https://doi.org/10.1175/JCLI-D-26-0199.1)** investigates the factors driving changes in the variability and persistence of Antarctic sea ice anomalies over decadal timescales. Using coupled Earth system model simulations, the authors show that internal climate variability can produce changes comparable to those observed in recent decades, without requiring anthropogenic forcing. They find that differences in Southern Annular Mode (SAM) patterns and wind-driven sea ice changes, along with stronger subsurface ocean temperature influences in the Weddell Sea, contribute to **periods of larger and longer-lasting sea ice anomalies**. These findings highlight the **important role of natural climate variability** in shaping recent Antarctic sea ice changes and provide **new insights into the mechanisms behind their persistence**.
