Led by **Mitch Bushuk**, this **[article](https://doi.org/10.1029/2026GL124043)** investigates the hypothesis that eddy-resolving climate models may improve the simulation of Antarctic sea ice trends. Using high-resolution model hierarchies from GFDL and NCAR alongside three generations of CMIP models, the study finds **no clear relationship between ocean or atmospheric resolution and Antarctic sea ice trends**, either over the historical period or the remainder of the 21st century. Instead, models that successfully reproduce the observed trend tend to exhibit large-amplitude multidecadal variability in the Southern Ocean, suggesting that **these models may be getting the “right answer for the wrong reasons.”**
